## Objective

Replace the Core Value and Bounce categories with one auditable Opportunity category. It should rank US-listed operating-company common stocks for a default six-month horizon using the exact fixed factor weights supplied by the product owner. The category must be broad enough to assess profitable value companies and higher-risk recovery/growth companies without requiring either thesis as a mandatory pattern.

The company report is a research workflow, not a promise that every field exists for every issuer. The system must attempt every applicable field, preserve every usable result when another source fails, retain source and period metadata, and state exactly which fields could not be verified. Missing evidence is never converted to zero, a PASS, an implied contract, or a made-up probability.

## Findings from the current repository

2. `lib/storage.ts` hashes both specs, writes `{core,bounce}` evaluations for every snapshot, computes two category counts, filters rows by a selected category, and re-evaluates historical rows against both engines.
3. `lib/scanner.ts` calculates both evaluations at snapshot-write time. The history stage has Bounce-specific candidate and enrichment paths; Core also affects which historic features are collected.
4. `app/page.tsx` exposes both category tabs, their separate copy/counts/model status, and strategy-specific cards. `app/scan-report.tsx`, `app/api/scan-report/route.ts`, portfolio summaries, audit/export endpoints, and tests also encode both strategy names.
5. Existing `Snapshot` evidence is materially narrower than the supplied investment prompt. It already supports sourced price/market cap, core XBRL financials, 12-month price history, dilution review, basic news, SEC filing metadata and some insider purchases. It does not yet provide a complete eight-quarter financial bridge, normalized earnings adjustments, contract ledger, competitor set, litigation/accounting review, governance facts, short interest/borrow costs, multiple fair-value methods, scenario model, or multi-timeframe technical indicators.
6. Production has previously returned SEC `data.sec.gov` HTTP 403 errors from the Railway environment. The current fallback architecture can use dated SEC Frames only for fields/tags in its curated set; this is not equivalent to reliable per-company Company Facts coverage.
7. Current visible claims are diagnostic, not predictive. The historical dataset is not complete point-in-time, lacks delisted-company coverage, and does not validate outcome probabilities. That limitation remains a hard release gate.

## Product contract

### Universe and safe membership

- One category: **Opportunity Radar**. Favorites and portfolio remain personal utility views, not investment categories.
- Default universe: operating-company common shares on supported US national exchanges. Keep ADRs only when identity/security metadata proves the instrument and filing mapping. Exclude ETFs, funds, preferreds, warrants, rights, units, debt, and other non-common instruments.
- The prior Core and Bounce economic tests become scored evidence. Profitability, EV/S, severe drawdown, distance from 52-week low, dilution, and MA30W are not conjunctive category gates.
- Membership safety requires a valid security identity, a positive dated price, a positive dated market cap, and evidenced 20-session median dollar volume above the operational floor. There is no market-cap minimum or maximum: small and micro caps remain in scope. The $150k median-dollar-volume screen is an operational tradability floor and must be disclosed as such. Missing/stale safety evidence yields `needs research`, not a fabricated exclusion.
- Ranking is distinct from eligibility. A company may appear in the single category as `needs research`; only candidates meeting the evidence-completeness threshold enter the ordered shortlist. Every company and reason remains inspectable in the scan report.

### Fixed score (100 points)

| Factor | Weight | Intended evidence |
|---|---:|---|
| Price versus fair value | 25 | Independently computed conservative/base/upside value ranges; fully diluted shares, debt, unrestricted cash, normalized cash flow and appropriate methods; peer multiples are corroboration, never the sole estimate |
| Growth catalysts in horizon | 20 | Dated filings/IR/news events, contract status and conversion, launch/approval/capacity milestones, earnings dates; obligation and probability are evidence labels, not invented certainty |
| Financial strength and liquidity | 15 | Cash availability, debt/maturities/covenants where disclosed, cash runway, working capital, liquidity and financing needs |
| Earnings and cash-flow quality | 12 | Eight quarters and three years where available; GAAP/non-GAAP bridge; recurring versus one-off earnings; operating cash conversion, capex and SBC |
| Competitive position and business quality | 10 | Product stage, customer/supplier concentration, market/industry evidence, peers, switching/IP/regulatory advantages and substitution risks |
| Risks and downside protection | 10 | Dilution instruments, funding/going-concern signs, customer/geographic/vendor concentration, litigation/accounting/control issues, short interest and measurable downside cases |
| Management, governance and alignment | 5 | Filed ownership/compensation/related-party/board evidence; code-P open-market Form 4 buys distinguished from grants, vesting, tax and planned sales |
| Chart and entry timing | 3 | Completed daily/weekly/monthly trend, relative strength, volume, gap and technically meaningful levels; intraday only when the user’s horizon needs it |
| **Total** | **100** | Fixed for all issuers and report runs |

Each factor score is 0–10 with dated source records and an explanation. Weighted points equal `score / 10 * weight`. The final displayed score is the sum of evidenced weighted points on the fixed 100-point denominator; factor coverage is displayed separately. Do not renormalize missing factors to 100. Do not state that the score is a probability or expected return. **Resolved ranking policy (spec v0.2.0): all eight weighted factors and 100% evidence coverage are required to rank.** The prior five-factor/82% proposal could allow missing competition, management, and timing evidence while still calling a result ranked; that conflicts with the user's all-factor comparison objective. Low coverage stays visible as `needs research`. Risk tolerance is recorded as analysis context and does not alter fixed comparative weights or factor scores; a later action recommendation may apply it separately.

Risk tolerance and investment period are report inputs. They may change scenario ranges, downside interpretation and entry language, but may not change the fixed factor weights or rewrite the underlying source facts. The market-wide default view is six months and medium tolerance until the owner chooses a different default.

## Data source research and implications

- SEC EDGAR `data.sec.gov` offers free unauthenticated submissions and standardized company facts; official bulk ZIPs are republished nightly. SEC data omits issuer-specific custom tags from standardized Company Facts, does not provide every requested qualitative fact, and automated access must use a descriptive User-Agent and comply with fair-access controls. The existing Railway 403 makes an explicit provider-health and fallback path necessary. [SEC EDGAR API guide](https://www.sec.gov/search-filings/edgar-application-programming-interfaces)
- SEC fair-access guidance states a 10 requests/second ceiling across automated requests. Bulk/scheduled retrieval plus cached issuer facts is preferable to unbounded per-ticker fan-out. [SEC rate-control notice](https://www.sec.gov/filergroup/announcements-old/new-rate-control-limits)
- Nasdaq Data Link documents historical and real-time/delayed market-data products, but its official site describes these as product subscriptions. It cannot be treated as a free broad-coverage fallback without checking the dataset-specific entitlement and redistribution terms. [Nasdaq Data Link overview](https://www.nasdaq.com/products/data/data-link/api), [Data Link documentation](https://docs.data.nasdaq.com/)
- Alpha Vantage currently advertises 25 requests/day for its general free service; real-time and 15-minute-delayed US equities data are premium. That quota is not sufficient for a thousands-of-symbol scanner. [Alpha Vantage support](https://www.alphavantage.co/support/), [market-data FAQ](https://www.alphavantage.co/support/#api-key)
- Financial Modeling Prep's current Basic plan advertises 250 calls/day, EOD historical data, and personal-use terms; its pricing page says redistribution/display requires a specific data-display and licensing agreement. Do not make it the underlying public-category source. [FMP pricing](https://site.financialmodelingprep.com/developer/docs/pricing)
- MCP/data-source audit: the configured tool catalog exposes Financial Datasets market-data tools, but its official terms say each API or hosted-MCP call is billable. The current official pricing is $20 for 1,000 pay-as-you-go credits, $200/month for Build, and $2,000/month for Scale; the published price page reserves product data-redistribution rights for Scale, while the terms allow other plans only under separate written agreement. The plugin is therefore neither free nor a rights-cleared runtime feed for this public app. I did not call it, to avoid incurring a charge. No other finance-data MCP source is present in the current tool catalog. The field registry labels this connector `paid-or-licensed`. [Financial Datasets pricing](https://www.financial-datasets.ai/pricing), [Financial Datasets terms](https://www.financial-datasets.ai/terms-of-use)
- OpenBB is an open-source provider orchestration platform, not a free market-data grant: its provider documentation says availability depends on upstream vendors, and many providers require credentials or subscriptions. It is not a suitable Cloudflare Edge runtime dependency; the existing TypeScript provider layer is the better fit, while OpenBB can serve as a local research cross-check. [OpenBB provider documentation](https://docs.openbb.co/odp/python/extensions/providers), [OpenBB repository](https://github.com/OpenBB-finance/OpenBB)
- EdgarTools is an open-source Python SEC-data library that can support local filing/facts workflows and requires a descriptive SEC identity while respecting request limits. It does not repair an SEC-egress block by itself or add unreported/custom non-XBRL facts; its Python runtime and multi-gigabyte offline workflow do not fit the current Worker deployment. [EdgarTools repository](https://github.com/dgunning/edgartools), [SEC compliance guidance](https://edgartools.readthedocs.io/en/stable/resources/sec-compliance/)
- Applicable biotech/regulatory milestones have primary public APIs: ClinicalTrials.gov API v2 and openFDA. These are conditional sources; a trial registration or regulator database row is not proof of efficacy, approval, revenue, or issuer identity until explicitly reconciled. [ClinicalTrials.gov API](https://clinicaltrials.gov/data-api/about-api), [openFDA API catalog](https://open.fda.gov/apis/)
- FRED is useful for macro series, and ALFRED supports vintage-aware observations, but API calls require a registered key and individual series can carry third-party use restrictions. It is a supplemental macro context source, not a company-data provider. [FRED API documentation](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/docs/api/fred/), [FRED terms](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/docs/api/terms_of_use.html)
- FINRA publishes official short-interest data twice per month for exchange-listed and OTC securities, with up to five rolling years in its interactive grid, historical downloads, and API. The API requires a registered Public credential, and FINRA says individual public credentials are not intended for commercial use at this time; use in the deployed product therefore requires a rights review. This data is position data as of settlement dates, not daily short-sale volume; borrow cost and shares available are separate fields. [FINRA equity short-interest catalog](https://www.finra.org/finra-data/browse-catalog/equity-short-interest), [FINRA API documentation](https://developer.finra.org/docs)
- Official short-interest source follow-up (2026-10-01): Nasdaq Trader describes a public per-issue lookup and rolling 12-month history for Nasdaq-covered issues, refreshed twice monthly; the complete consolidated bulk report is subscription-based. NYSE Group lists its semi-monthly history as a market-data product. The registry now records Nasdaq's page as a research link with unverified automated-use/redistribution rights and NYSE as paid/licensed; neither is represented as free universal ingestion. FINRA/Nasdaq position data must not be replaced by daily short-sale volume. [Nasdaq Trader short interest](https://www.nasdaqtrader.com/trader.aspx?id=ShortInterest), [Nasdaq Short Interest Report access](https://www.nasdaq.com/products/data/equities/short-interest), [NYSE Group Short Interest](https://www.nyse.com/data-products/catalog/nyse-group-short-interest), [FINRA distinction between position and volume](https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/notices/21-19)
- EDGAR follow-up (2026-10-01): the SEC documents keyless submissions and standardized XBRL APIs and nightly bulk ZIPs, updated through the day. Its newer full-text search supports filing search beyond the limited company submission index, but a human-readable search surface is not automatically a documented bulk API or a reuse grant for third-party material inside filings. Standard Company Facts deliberately aggregates standard taxonomies; company-specific extension concepts and disclosures still require filing-level retrieval, mapping, and validation. [SEC EDGAR API guide](https://www.sec.gov/search-filings/edgar-application-programming-interfaces), [SEC EDGAR full-text search](https://www.sec.gov/edgar/search/), [SEC developer resources](https://www.sec.gov/about/developer-resources)
- GitHub/MCP/library follow-up: open-source data adapters and MCP wrappers do not confer upstream data rights. The repository search surfaced personal-use scraper/tooling projects and provider aggregators, not a demonstrated free, rights-cleared source that can fill this product's US equity quotes, full financial history, short-borrow availability, and news/analyst datasets for every listed company. Adding those wrappers would add dependencies without repairing coverage or rights. Official source and subscription terms remain the acceptance boundary; no paid/quota-consuming MCP/API was called during this review.
- Cloudflare D1 free tier currently includes 5m rows read/day, 100k rows written/day, and a 500MB database limit. The D1 limit is operationally relevant because broad fact storage and frequent full-table summaries could break app availability; indexed queries, bounded writes, payload compaction and query metrics are required. [D1 pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/d1/platform/pricing/), [D1 limits](https://developers.cloudflare.com/d1/platform/limits/)
- Inference from these official contracts: no one free, rights-cleared provider can supply every requested real-time quote, detailed company fact, contract/legal event, analyst/borrow metric, transcript, competitor fact and chart series for every stock. The design must implement field-by-field source attempts, tiered refresh, evidence provenance, non-blocking partial results, and explicit unavailable states. Scraping sites with no documented rights is not an acceptable way to claim complete coverage.

## Waves, work parts, and acceptance criteria

### Wave 0 — Audit and contract (this wave)

1. Map both current strategy contracts, scan stages, DB JSON, endpoints, reports, cards, portfolio summaries, tests, and production gates.
2. Inventory every requested field and classify it: broad-scan field, on-demand dossier field, conditional disclosure, restricted/unavailable field.
3. Research official/free source terms and quotas; inspect relevant skills and install useful local tooling skills.
4. Publish this plan and identify baseline behavior and data limitations.

Acceptance: code-path inventory is complete; sources link to official terms; no implementation claim suggests free data can guarantee disclosure.

### Wave 1 — Unified rating contract

1. Add one canonical `opportunity` specification with the supplied eight weights and horizon/risk defaults.
2. Add typed field-level evidence, factor-level rationale, score coverage, source conflict, and decision states (`ranked`, `needs research`, `excluded`).
3. Derive scores only from validated evidence values; preserve unavailable factors as null and fixed-denominator points.
4. Add boundary tests for weights, nulls, invalid evidence, stale/future evidence, source conflicts, no rescoring on risk tolerance, and ranking eligibility threshold.

Acceptance: factor weights sum exactly to 100; no `core`/`bounce` gate semantics enter the new evaluator; all new evaluator tests pass.

### Wave 2 — Field/source coverage map and resilient retrieval

1. Create a machine-readable source registry per field: primary/fallback, free/auth requirement, rate limit, delay, license/redistribution status, evidence time semantics, parser contract, and failure policy.
2. Make identity/security-master resolution deterministic (CIK/LEI/ticker/exchange/share class/history), including ADR and ticker-change handling.
3. Rework SEC acquisition around company-level cached facts/submissions and bulk datasets; repair production 403 diagnosis/fallback without exceeding SEC fairness controls.
4. Implement field-independent provider fan-out with per-provider timeouts, bounded concurrency, dedupe, retry-after/backoff, freshness TTL, and partial success.
5. Add parsers for all supported standard US-GAAP/IFRS facts, period alignment, fiscal quarters, amendments/restatements, split-adjusted shares, Form 4/144/13D/13G, filing/events, and source conflict detection.
6. Make fields with no universal free/right-cleared feed remain explicitly unavailable and list the correct manual/source path.

Acceptance: provider matrix test covers all requested fields; replay tests prove one provider outage does not erase successful siblings; values cannot cross incompatible fiscal periods/currencies/share classes; every output value has provenance or is null.

### Wave 3 — Broad scan and data-quality automation

1. Fetch compact market-wide common-stock quotes and candidate metrics in bounded resumable batches.
2. Separate scanner snapshots from larger on-demand dossiers; avoid storing large history arrays or filing documents in every row.
3. Schedule filings/financial refresh based on filing events and age; price refresh by market session; cache unchanged XBRL by accession/hash.
4. Add per-field coverage/freshness dashboards, data-run status, provider-health page, queue age/retry controls, stale-data warnings, and scan cost/row-write telemetry.
5. Add reproducible sample-based validation against SEC filings for US/foreign issuers, banks/insurers, biotech/pre-revenue, REIT-like edge cases, and actions/restatements.

Acceptance: full-universe runs finish in bounded CPU/memory/provider calls and preserve partial-run visibility; all per-field coverage counts reconcile to stored rows; failures do not replace the last usable run.

### Wave 4 — Full company research dossier

1. Implement eight-quarter/three-year financial tables with consistent fiscal periods and GAAP/Non-GAAP reconciliation where published.
2. Implement balance-sheet, debt maturity, cash runway, dilution instruments, SBC and cash conversion calculations.
3. Extract candidate contracts/catalysts from filings and official IR; classify binding/conditional/nonbinding/realized revenue and date every item.
4. Add management, ownership and insider transaction review using primary filings; distinguish transaction codes and 10b5-1 plans.
5. Add peers/industry/competition using a sourced taxonomy and explain comparability limits; no unsupported market-share estimates.
6. Add litigation, auditor/control, short interest and borrow modules only where a free rights-cleared source is available; otherwise disclose coverage gap.
7. Add multiple valuation approaches and three scenarios with assumption tables. Never use analyst consensus alone as fair value.
8. Store user-selected period, risk tolerance, current holdings/cost basis as report context without leaking one user’s portfolio into another user’s result.

Acceptance: synthetic and real-company fixtures validate each section; every material statement links to dated source evidence; no report claims a complete review when a module is absent.

### Wave 5 — One-category product/UI migration

1. Replace Core/Bounce navigation with one Opportunity view and one ranked list.
2. Add in-view filters for `ranked`, `needs research`, and `excluded`; these are states within the single workflow, not additional investment categories.
3. Present weighted score, evidence coverage, confidence, price timestamp, fair-value range, near-term catalyst calendar and primary blockers.
4. Keep one company card and one full report entry path; remove obsolete two-strategy descriptions, gates, validation labels and styling.
5. Update scan report, audit/export, notifications, offline state, favorites, portfolio scoring metadata, Arabic strings, accessible mobile and screen-reader behavior.

Acceptance: RTL, keyboard, touch, 375px/768px/1024px/1440px browser checks; no old category labels or routes in active UI/API; user data and favorites persist.

### Wave 6 — Historical validation and promotion gate

1. Build a point-in-time dataset with delisted names, historical fundamentals known at each date, point-in-time security identity, corporate actions, and no future-data leakage.
2. Freeze the supplied weights and define outcome by selected horizon, benchmark, total-return dividends, trading costs, slippage, liquidity and delisting returns.
3. Use chronological training/validation/locked-test splits, firm-held-out tests, multiple-testing correction, bootstrap confidence intervals, and sector/size/risk cohort diagnostics.
4. Compare score and simple baselines; do not fit alternative weights unless the owner explicitly changes the fixed-weight requirement.
5. Keep result as diagnostic unless the minimum sample, calibration and out-of-sample gates pass.

Acceptance: reproducible backtest manifest, leakage checks, baseline comparison, complete delisting treatment, locked holdout, confidence intervals, and sign-off that no predictive probability is displayed without calibration.

### Wave 7 — Backward-compatible migration and release

1. Version/hash the unified engine; read old `{core,bounce}` evaluations and re-evaluate old snapshots lazily as `opportunity`.
2. Preserve favorites, portfolio, scan history, saved reports and all old source evidence. Keep a rollback release and do not delete historical rows.
3. Release through a shadow-read period, reconcile old/new counts and sample explanations, then switch UI/API default to the unified view.
4. Retire legacy engines only after users can inspect previous run reports and no active service relies on old keys.
5. Verify GitHub commit, Railway deployment, live API responses, fresh scan status, and browser rendering before closure.

Acceptance: migration is idempotent/restartable; row counts/payload samples and user-owned rows match; rollback is demonstrated; production starts and completes a current scan with accurate coverage.
